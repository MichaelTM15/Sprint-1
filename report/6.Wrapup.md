## Performance metrics
The first metric we chose to compare was the test accuracy. This serves as the most straightforward measure of the models' predictive performance. Next, we chose to look at ROC curves and more importantly the One-vs-Rest AUC values. These take into account the class specific performances and combine the information given from the recall and precision. Finally, we chose the multi-class log-loss as it evaluates how well calibrated your probability distributions are across multiple classes, which ends up providing a more rigorous comparison compared to accuracy score. 

For a classification problem with three outcomes, guessing randomly would give a log-loss score of log(3) which equals 1.0986. Also, predicting only one class gives a maximum test accuracy of 45.49%, in the case of Home Win. Now, we look at the values of these performance metrics for our 4 models.

## Interpretation

However, that is not to discredit the other models since they all performed relatively similarly. It appears that the non-parametric models didn't have any noticeable advantage over the MLR; this may be due to the data having a simple decision boundary.
