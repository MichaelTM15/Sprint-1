# Wrapup

## Performance metrics
The first metric we chose to compare was the test accuracy. This serves as the most straightforward measure of the models' predictive performance. Next, we chose to look at ROC curves and more importantly the One-vs-Rest AUC values. These take into account the class specific performances and combine the information given from the recall and precision. Finally, we chose the multi-class log-loss as it evaluates how well calibrated your probability distributions are across multiple classes, as well as being computable for all of our chosen models unlike AIC, ROC, etc. This provides a more rigorous comparison compared to accuracy score. 

Now, we look at the values of these performance metrics for our 4 models.


## Table

| ----- | ----------------- | -------- | -------- | -------- | -------- |
| MLR   | 60.56             | 0.8620   | 0.807    | 0.823    | 0.637    |
| RF    | 59.61             | 0.8839   | 0.798    | 0.811    | 0.620    |
| XGB   | 60.01             | 0.8646   | 0.810    | 0.820    | 0.630    |
| SVM   | 60.14             | 0.8824   | 0.794    | 0.815    | 0.600    |

## Interpretation 
From the table above, we can see that the best performing model for test accuracy was Multinomial Logistic Regression. Predicting only one class would give a maximum test accuracy of 45.49%, in the case of Home Win. The model performed to at least a reasonable baseline standard. 

It appears that the non-parametric models didn't have any noticeable advantage over the MLR; this may be due to the data having a simple decision boundary. Therefore, for this task, as the MLR requires the least computation, it could be considered the best choice. 

The table highlights AUC_Draw values to be barely above the random guess AUC. This is consistent across all findings which suggests that all models had difficulties in classifying Draws. The models tended to avoid predicting Draws, as seen in the recall scores. This could relate back to the limitations within the data; the predictors may not have been informative enough to distinguish between Wins and Draws. 

For a classification problem with three outcomes, guessing randomly would give a log-loss score of log(3) which equals 1.0986. All of the log-loss scores are within 0.0219 of each other, so this only acts as a minor indication in our comparison. This further supports our claim of using the least complex model, which is MLR. 

Another conclusion we drew was that the models had a higher accuracy when looking at the test set compared to the training set. Linking this back to what we learned about how they handle Draws, we believe a possible explanation for this improved performance is the smaller frequency of Draws in the Premier League.
